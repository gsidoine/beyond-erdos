# P026 — Do four-cycles generate the prime-sum graph cycle space?

<!-- DOSSIER-SUMMARY -->
> **Research question:** Do the four-cycles of the bipartite prime-sum graph generate its entire binary cycle space for every n, and how efficiently can its cycles be filled by squares?
> **Formulation:** candidate. **Novelty:** not certified (specialist source check needed).
> **Verified/known progress:** Exact finite-field elimination gives vanishing square-filled first homology for n up to 60; no proof for all n.
> **Finite evidence:** Exhaustive enumeration of all four-cycles and their GF(2) boundary span; reproducible standard-library code.

**Area:** graph theory, combinatorial topology, additive number theory. **Status:** open candidate, not certified historically novel.

## Exact model

For n ≥ 2 let B_n have bipartition L=R=[n], with (i,j) an edge exactly when i+j is prime. Define the binary cycle space Z_1(B_n;F_2), and let Q_n be the linear span over F_2 of the edge-boundaries of every *four-cycle* (K_2,2) in B_n.

P026 asks whether

$$
Z_1(B_n;\mathbb F_2)=Q_n\qquad\text{for every }n\ge2.
$$

Equivalently, attach a two-cell to every four-cycle and ask whether the resulting square 2-complex has H_1(-;F_2)=0. If yes, find the asymptotic worst-case **square filling length** of an arbitrary simple cycle, measured by the minimum number of four-cycles whose symmetric difference equals it. These are different questions: generation does not bound the optimal filling length uniformly.

## Rigorous baseline

For any finite graph with v vertices, e edges and c connected components, the cycle space has dimension e−v+c. Thus the assertion can be *decided exactly for each n* by a binary rank computation on four-cycle incidence vectors. The four-cycle condition requires primes at four **correlated** sums i+j, i+j', i'+j, i'+j', so arbitrary dense-bipartite intuition does not replace arithmetic analysis.

The [exact rank checker](../../experiments/P026_prime_sum_square_homology.py) yields

| n | edges | cycle-space dimension | four-cycles | remaining H1 dimension |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 12 | 51 | 28 | 76 | 0 |
| 20 | 113 | 74 | 320 | 0 |
| 40 | 401 | 322 | 4086 | 0 |
| 60 | 843 | 724 | 17144 | 0 |

All sizes 2≤n≤60 are checked, not merely these displayed sizes. The result is finite evidence; the universal assertion is **not** established.

## Precise prior-art risk

- [Prime Sum Graphs and the Induced Trees They Contain (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00373-025-02999-2): arithmetic prime-sum graph structure already has substantial theory; here a *bipartite two-copy* version and square-boundary space are specified.
- [Ohsugi–Hibi, Toric Ideals Generated by Quadratic Binomials (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1006/jabr.1999.7918): square-cycle generators and quadratic edge toric ideals are **standard concepts**. A sufficient chordal-bipartite criterion may subsume parts of the question.
- [Cycle spaces and graph homology](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0195-6698(83)80018-3): generation of cycle spaces is classical, not invented here.

**Priority unresolved:** investigate known theorems on toric ideals of bipartite prime-sum graphs, whether the graph is chordal bipartite for particular n, and whether a general cycle-generation argument follows directly from prime gaps. Do not advertise the square complex or binary-cycle approach as new.

## Falsification / follow-up

Find an n with positive H1 dimension, or derive a universal square-filling algorithm. Independently distinguish *F_2-homological generation*, *integral cycle generation* and *simply-connectedness*: none automatically implies the others.
