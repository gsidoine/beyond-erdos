## Conclusive predecessor boundaries

- **P007:** Panma–Rochanakul's 2021 *Prime-Graceful Graphs* treats full distinct-gap coprime path labeling. The candidate concerns the *maximum partial* gap spectrum; the complete graceful case must not be advertised as original. [Source](https://thaijmath.com/index.php/thaijmath/article/view/1264).
- **P025:** Erdős–Freud–Hegyvári (1983) proved the asymptotic minimum adjacent-lcm bottleneck for *linear* permutations. Only the cyclic constraint remains distinct. [Original paper](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1983-02.pdf).
- **P035:** Espinosa-García–Montejano–Roldán-Pensado–Suárez prove the classical Sidon–Ramsey leading asymptotic. The joint divisibility-antichain condition is not covered by that theorem as stated. [Theorem 1 (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40590-024-00676-7).
- **P029:** Permutation X-rays are a classical discrete-tomography object; the prime restriction does not make the underlying X-ray construction new. [Bebeacua et al. (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.endm.2005.05.063).
- **P030:** The same sum-and-difference primality constraints already occur in circular arrangements. Such finite arrangements can imply matching witnesses, but do not resolve every even order. [OEIS A227050](https://oeis.org/A227050).
- **P037:** Unboundedness of the deficit is recorded as a consequence of Pintz's prime-gap theorem, not as a new conjecture. [Pintz (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-22240-0_17).
- **P038:** The leading set-pair bound comes from Bollobás's inequality; the prime-product restriction raises a finer quantitative question. [Set-pair literature](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0963548323000044).
- **P041:** The hypercube accessible-path theorem is imported from Hegarty–Martinsson, *Annals of Applied Probability* **24** (2014), 1375–1395. Its application to squarefree divisor intervals is not presented as a new hypercube theorem. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1214/13-AAP949).

## Outstanding source-equivalence risks

The closest theorem statements require especially careful comparison for **P009** (Maillet/prime differences), **P015** (arbitrary parity-color triangulation flips), **P018** (group-labelled matroid basis fibers), **P020** (structured p-adic Hankel cokernels), **P026** (square-cycle generation and toric ideals), **P029** (tomographic Markov bases), **P032** (prime-difference existence), **P035** (simultaneous Sidon/antichain colorings), **P039** (bilinear near-prime matching) and **P041** (poset accessibility).

Two distinct divisibility models occur in the catalogue: **P017** retains integers independently and asks for the height of the induced divisibility order; **P041** keeps all integers, randomly orders them, and asks for the length of an increasing divisor subsequence. Neither formulation subsumes the other merely by referring to a “random divisibility chain.”
