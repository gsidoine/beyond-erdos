## Definitions of the evidence categories

1. **Resolved internally**: a manuscript or complete proof is present; originality and refereeing remain separate.
2. **Reframe**: a recognizable predecessor has settled or subsumed a main form, and only a narrower surviving question should be retained.
3. **Specify**: the proposed limit, centering, or model is insufficiently precise to assess as an open problem.
4. **Source gate**: a strong overlap with an established mathematical area creates a substantial risk of duplication; require a theorem-level comparison before promotion.
5. **Priority**: sharply formulated and accompanied by a theorem, serious counterexample, or nontrivial structural evidence, but **not** certified new.
6. **Incubate**: valid exploratory direction but not yet a separate flagship-level research question.

No entry currently has the label **historically verified new formulation**. An apparently new conjunction of two familiar constraints is not an originality certificate. Even when an exact formula is proved, it may be known under graph switchings, Cayley sums, additive energy, matroid reconfiguration, or poset topology terminology.

## High-confidence known/overlap distinctions

- **P007:** full prime-graceful path existence is a known published theorem; only the refined extremal *partial* gap-spectrum question may survive. **Not a new full gracefulness conjecture.**
- **P025:** the linear adjacent-lcm extremum has an earlier asymptotic theorem (Erdős–Freud–Hegyvári); the cyclic condition requires its own comparison. **Do not market the linear case as new.**
- **P009 and P032:** constructions and diameter claims are tied to prime differences and may hide major unresolved analytic-number-theory hypotheses; do not promote a computation to an eventual assertion.
- **P029:** exact permutation X-ray fibers belong to discrete tomography, with extensive reconstruction and trade-move theory. The prime restriction alone may not create a separate new theorem.
- **P031 and P035:** these differ fundamentally. P031 requires *pairwise coprimality* inside a single Sidon set; P035 requires *divisibility incomparability* inside each color class, a weaker multiplicative constraint, but covers all integers by coloring.
- **P033 and P034:** connectedness under three prime-sum labels is distinct from 2-vertex-connectivity under four labels. Yet both are in the already established prime-sum Hamiltonicity literature and should be compared at the level of theorems, not just titles.
- **P010:** internally proved, submitted to *Integers*; **not open** and not automatically accepted or historically original.
- **P020 and P027:** serious internal proof packages exist; present general laws remain unproved. Distinguish known building blocks from new consequences. Their high priority is based on mathematical development, not novelty certification.

## P035 red-team issue: a dependency requiring independent verification

The new P035 dossier uses the claim that **any interval of M consecutive integers admits a partition into (1+o(1))sqrt(M) Sidon classes**. This must be confirmed at a primary source with the precise **integer Sidon** convention (diagonal sums included), not merely deduced from the existence of *one* size-(1+o(1))sqrt(M) Sidon subset. The two assertions are **not logically equivalent**.

The lower bound for P035 follows from the classical Sidon maximum-size estimate. The dyadic-upper-bound *method* is correct conditional on a uniform sharp Sidon-partition theorem. **Until that theorem is located and checked, mark the numeric leading upper coefficient 1+sqrt(2) as source-dependent, not independently certified by this audit.** An unconditional but weaker O(sqrt(M)) coloring bound can be obtained by finite-field cyclic Sidon constructions if their covering statement is explicitly proved, and would still imply chi_PS(N)=Theta(sqrt(N)).

Source nearest to the topic: [Sidon–Ramsey number literature](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2025.07.002). The term *Sidon–Ramsey* names integer interval partitions into Sidon color classes; use it in future source searches, rather than only *Sidon subsets*.

## External databases and source cautions

- [Erdős Problems](https://www.erdosproblems.com/) and the community [teorth/erdosproblems structured dataset](https://github.com/teorth/erdosproblems) are **reference databases**, not proof that missing formulations are novel. The teorth database explicitly lists formalized statements, current source status and OEIS correspondences.
- [Open Problem Garden](https://www.openproblemgarden.org/) is useful for terminology but source age and status need individual verification.
- [Recent precise Sidon upper bounds, Carter–O'Bryant–Tao (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36207) concern the **maximum size of a single Sidon subset**, not the chromatic number of the Sidon-conflict hypergraph. Do not substitute one theorem for the other.
- [Bailleul–Riblet (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03181) supplies arbitrary-set Sidon subset estimates for the prime-set baseline in P031, **not** the sharp density conjecture for pairwise-coprime Sidon sets.
