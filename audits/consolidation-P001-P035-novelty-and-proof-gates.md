## Definitions of the evidence categories

1. **Resolved internally**: a manuscript or complete proof is present; originality and refereeing remain separate.
2. **Reframe**: a recognizable predecessor has settled or subsumed a main form, and only a narrower surviving question should be retained.
3. **Specify**: the proposed limit, centering, or model is insufficiently precise to assess as an open problem.
4. **Source gate**: a strong overlap with an established mathematical area creates a substantial risk of duplication; require a theorem-level comparison before promotion.
5. **Priority**: sharply formulated and accompanied by a theorem, serious counterexample, or nontrivial structural evidence, but **not** certified new.
6. **Incubate**: valid exploratory direction but not yet a separate flagship-level research question.

No entry currently has the label **historically verified new formulation**. An apparently new conjunction of two familiar constraints is not an originality certificate. Even when an exact formula is proved, it may be known under graph switchings, Cayley sums, additive energy, matroid reconfiguration, or poset topology terminology.

**Incubation means:** retain the question and finite artifacts, but suspend headline novelty/open claims and routine computational expansion until a specific promotion gate is met. It is *not* mathematical rejection. Promotion requires (i) a precise theorem/conjecture with bounded scope, (ii) a substantive result or obstruction that cannot be read off a standard theorem, and (iii) a theorem-level prior-art comparison. Incubating entries should have a named next decisive experiment or proof target, a clear last verified baseline, and a cross-reference to any stronger sibling problem.

## P035 primary-source correction: the Sidon partition theorem is verified

The sharp **classical** Sidon partition statement used in P035 *is* backed by a primary research source: Espinosa-García, Montejano, Roldán-Pensado and Suárez (2024), *Sidon–Ramsey and B_h-Ramsey numbers*, [DOI 10.1007/s40590-024-00676-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40590-024-00676-7), **Theorem 1**, establishes

$$
\mathrm{SR}(k)=k^2+o(k^2),
$$

where $\mathrm{SR}(k)$ is the *first* interval length that cannot be partitioned into $k$ integer Sidon sets (including repeated-element sums). Inverting the monotone parameter gives the precise needed statement: **the minimum Sidon partition count on $[M]$ is $(1+o(1))\sqrt M$**. Translation preserves the Sidon property, so the result works on arbitrary intervals of $M$ consecutive integers.

Therefore the P035 dyadic upper bound

$$
\chi_{\mathrm{PS}}(N)\le(1+\sqrt2+o(1))\sqrt N
$$

is a **valid combination of an established sharp partition theorem and a simple multiplicative-antichain decomposition**, assuming the dyadic asymptotic/tail calculation recorded in the dossier. It is **not independently novel**. The remaining originality question is whether simultaneous *primitive Sidon coloring* has been studied, perhaps under constrained Sidon–Ramsey terminology. Do not claim that adding divisibility is sufficient to establish novelty.

Be especially careful: the sharp finite Sidon *set size* bound in Carter–O'Bryant–Tao (2026) and Theorem 1 on Sidon–Ramsey numbers are **different theorems**. The former does not by itself imply the latter.

## External databases and source cautions

- [Erdős Problems](https://www.erdosproblems.com/) and the community [teorth/erdosproblems structured dataset](https://github.com/teorth/erdosproblems) are **reference databases**, not proof that missing formulations are novel. The teorth database explicitly lists formalized statements, current source status and OEIS correspondences.
