# Discovery pass 4 — literature and adversarial audit

**Date:** 2026-10-28  
**Scope:** P009 and P010 candidates, targeted follow-up on P005/P006, and discarded questions. **This is not a comprehensive novelty certification.** Sources and claims are distinguished below.

## P009 — Two-value prime-sum pairings

**Question:** Are all $[2n]$ prime-sum perfect matchings realizable with at most two distinct prime sums (selecting a suitable matching)?

**Firm prior art:**
- [Greenfield & Greenfield, *Some Problems of Combinatorial Number Theory Related to Bertrand's Postulate* (1998)](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/green.html): existence of prime-sum pairings, without the two-value restriction.
- [OEIS A000341](https://oeis.org/A000341): number of prime-sum pairings, not minimum palette.
- [OEIS A202472](https://oeis.org/A202472): exact count of *short interval* prime-difference pairs $(q,p)$ with $q<2n$ and $p-q=2n$. These are the precise sufficient certificates used by the elementary two-prime construction in P009.
- [Pintz, *On the difference of primes* (2012)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.0149): work on gaps between integers representable as differences of primes. The original Maillet conjecture (1905) that every even number is a difference of two primes remains unresolved, but the P009 certificate asks for the stronger **bounded smaller prime** $q<2n$.
- [2018 question on pairing with all *distinct* prime sums](https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/3031150/pair-up-1-2n-that-the-sums-of-each-pair-are-different-primes): this studies an opposite/max-spectrum property, related to P001.

**Overlaps and warning:** The sufficiency condition $q,q+2n$ prime is an existing prime-pair problem **in OEIS**. No novelty is claimed for the prime-pair condition. The literature search did not locate the two-*color matching* optimization as a theorem or conjecture in these sources. A solution of the bounded-prime-difference conjecture would imply P009, but P009 could be weaker: a two-color matching may use primes with difference not equal to $2n$.

**Computational test:** A simple deterministic sieve constructed explicit two-color witnesses for every $2\le n\le100{,}000$, with no counterexamples. A one-color witness handles $n=1$. The largest minimum qualifying $q$ observed was $379$ at $n=90{,}407$. These are finite tests, not proof for unbounded $n$.

**Verdict:** candidate number-theoretic rephrasing with a clear prime-pair sufficiency theorem; originality and unresolved status **unverified**. Moderate risk of overlap.

## P010 — Fewest coprime adjacent pairs in a permutation

**Question:** For permutations $(a_i)$ of $[n]$, determine the least number $h(n)$ of pairs $(a_i,a_{i+1})$ with $\gcd(a_i,a_{i+1})=1$. Is $h(n)\sim(7/12)n/\log n$?

**Closest historical paper:** P. Erdős, R. Freud and N. Hegyvári, [*Arithmetical properties of permutations of integers*](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1983-02.pdf), *Acta Math. Hung.* 41 (1983), 169–176, DOI [10.1007/BF01994075](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01994075). This studies gcd and lcm of adjacent permutation entries, but primarily their *magnitude* rather than the number of coprime transitions. This citation must be central to P010's history.

**Graph-theoretic host:** [*Non-Coprime Graph of Integers* (2020)](https://ijmttjournal.org/archive/ijmtt-v66i2p513), introducing a noncoprime graph (deleting isolated vertices) and studying graph invariants. The host relation $\gcd(a,b)>1$ is therefore **not new**. The natural connection to minimum *path cover* is standard graph theory; the arithmetic graph's exact asymptotic path-cover number was not located in our queries.

**Opposite extremal direction:** [OEIS A076220](https://oeis.org/A076220) counts permutations having *all* adjacent pairs coprime; unlike our minimum count.

**Internal mathematical results:** exact graph reformulation; elementary prime-isolate/degree-one lower bound

$$
h(n)\ge\pi(n)-\pi(n/2)+\tfrac12(\pi(n/2)-\pi(n/3)) + O(1)
=(7/12+o(1))n/\log n;
$$

elementary largest-prime-factor block upper bound $h(n)\le\pi(n)=(1+o(1))n/\log n$.

**Computational test:** full Hamiltonian MILP optima at $n=5,\ldots,22$ and selected $n\le200$. The lower bound is tight at $n=50,60,80,100,120,150,200$ and **not** tight at $n=9,21$. This motivated the *asymptotic* rather than an exact-for-all-$n$ question.

**Verdict:** a natural genuinely Erdősian arithmetic-permutation problem; exact count-asymptotic not located in targeted searches. High relevance but serious prior-art risk because of the 1983 paper and follow-ups.

## P005 — Coprime permutation switches (follow-up)

A targeted search using "coprime permutation transposition", "coprime matching Markov chain", "coprime matrix switch" and "gcd permutation Markov basis" did not locate a theorem on the precise switch graph in P005.

Closest first-party treatment: [Pomerance, *Coprime Permutations*](https://math.dartmouth.edu/~carlp/perm3.pdf) (2022) focuses on enumeration; general switching results are in [Dyer–Jerrum–Müller](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/111478/1/monotone.pdf).

**Counterexample to a tempting shortcut:** One universal row and one universal column in a bipartite graph do **not** guarantee switch connectivity. A 5-by-5 adjacency matrix (rows separated by semicolons)

$$
[11111;\ 10110;\ 11010;\ 11100;\ 10000]
$$

has two perfect matchings $(5,3,4,2,1)$ and $(5,4,2,3,1)$ (columns by row), and **neither admits any four-cycle switch**. The universal row and column are both index 1. This is a direct verified finite counterexample, not claimed to be a new theorem. Thus P005's universally adjacent index 1 alone is insufficient for a generic connectivity proof; prime-specific extra structure is essential.

## P006 — Sidon sets with no prime differences (follow-up)

A targeted search for "Sidon no prime differences", "all differences composite Golomb ruler", and "prime-free Golomb ruler" found classical [Sidon/Golomb ruler theory](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/SidonSet.html), but no direct asymptotic. This is **not** proof of novelty. Earlier $1/\sqrt2$ construction by dilating a Sidon set is elementary; a stronger construction or upper bound would make the work substantively more significant.

## Rejected as open / completely solved during discovery

**GCD difference-spectrum Hamiltonian path:** For every $n$, it is easy to order $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ such that each attainable gcd $d=1,\ldots,\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ occurs on some adjacent pair, with every other newly inserted bridge having gcd 1. Indeed edges $d\leftrightarrow 2d$ form vertex-disjoint doubling chains indexed by odd roots. Traverse each chain in increasing order and concatenate chains by increasing odd roots. The final (largest even) vertex of one chain and the next odd root $r+2$ are coprime because $\gcd(2^k r,r+2)=\gcd(r,r+2)=1$ for odd $r$. Thus the proposed spectrum-existence question is **trivial by construction**, not an open candidate.

**Caution:** The existence of many graceful/coprime arithmetic permutations has a large existing literature; problem creation needs an immediate construction/counterexample pass before promotion.
